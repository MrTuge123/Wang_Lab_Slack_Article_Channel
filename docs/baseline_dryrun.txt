*Overview:* Recent submissions cluster around three main themes: graph neural network methods for cancer grading and subtype classification, integration of multi‑omics data for variant interpretation and pharmacogenomics, and broader AI applications linking molecular design to clinical outcomes. Approximately a dozen papers apply graph attention or relational networks to histopathology or spatial transcriptomics, while another ten focus on multi‑omics fusion using GNNs. A recurring trend is the emphasis on model interpretability and clinical translation, reflected in studies that combine knowledge graphs with deep learning or that embed mechanistic models within AI pipelines. The highlighted papers (#1, #2, #3, #5) were chosen for their strong alignment with these directions, high relevance to graph‑based bioinformatics, and clear articulation of methodological advances.

*<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42809995/|RACR-MIL: Rank-aware contextual reasoning for weakly supervised grading of squamous cell carcinoma using whole slide images.>*
_Medical image analysis · top author: Ravishankar K. Iyer (last, h=63) · keywords: graph neural network · score 1.10_
Addressed the challenge of grading squamous cell carcinoma on whole‑slide images by introducing RACR‑MIL, an attention‑based multiple‑instance learning framework that uses a hybrid graph to capture local and non‑local tissue relationships and applies rank‑ordering constraints to the attention scores. Reported 3‑9% improvement in grading accuracy and up to 10% better tumor localization compared with prior methods, with pathologists noting increased grading efficiency in 60% of cases.

*<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42884114/|Artificial Intelligence in Photodynamic Therapy: From Molecular Design of Photosensitizers to Intelligent Delivery, Adaptive Dosimetry, and Personalized Prognostication of Results.>*
_International journal of molecular sciences · top author: David Aebisher (corresponding, h=34) · keywords: machine learning, deep learning, AI, artificial intelligence · score 1.02_
Reviewed how artificial intelligence, including graph neural networks and generative models, can support the full photodynamic therapy workflow—from in silico photosensitizer design and nanoformulation optimization to adaptive light dosing, real‑time monitoring, and outcome prediction. Emphasized integration of mechanistic photochemistry models with AI to move toward reproducible, personalized photomedicine, highlighting data quality, validation, and interpretability concerns.

*<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42862883/|Explainable/interpretable artificial intelligence and knowledge graph neural network for synthetic lethality prediction: overview, challenges, and future directions.>*
_Briefings in bioinformatics · top author: Noura Aherrahrou (last, h=6) · keywords: multi-omics, knowledge graph, graph neural network, GNN, AI, artificial intelligence · score 0.96_
Surveyed graph neural network approaches for synthetic lethality prediction that combine heterogeneous knowledge graphs with multi‑omics data, evaluating architectures for multi‑layered biological integration, model interpretability, and emerging generative AI contributions. Identified challenges in transparent reasoning and clinical trust, and outlined future directions for zero‑shot reasoning using large language models.

_Frontiers in Pharmacology · top author: Lei Guo (corresponding, h=38) · keywords: multi-omics, machine learning, deep learning · score 0.89_
